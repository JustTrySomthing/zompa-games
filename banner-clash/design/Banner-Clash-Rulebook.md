# Banner Clash
## Player rulebook and pre-build design • Edition 6

### How to play in a minute

Win three banners to win the match. The gold flag marks the battlefield that scores next. Take turns playing characters there to build strength, or send a character ahead to the next battlefield. The opening player acts first. Alternate one action at a time until each player has taken five open turns. Then each of you secretly picks one last character to help at the flag, or chooses Hold. Reveal together. Higher strength wins the banner. If the flag is tied, total your strength at the other two sites. If those totals also tie, you each earn a banner. Reaching three together is a draw.

Suppose your friend has a Hill Giant at the Bridge and you have a Stone Guard in hand. You can play the Guard at the Bridge to narrow the deficit, or send it ahead to the Watchtower. If you send it ahead, it will still be there when the flag arrives. You can see the upcoming battlefield before committing the card.

### Set up the table

1. Choose Solo, Online, or Pass & Play. Give each player an identical personal deck of 18 cards, two of each card listed below. Hands and draw order are private. Board pieces, current strengths, discards, and deck counts are public.
2. The three locations form a fixed line, Bridge — Watchtower — Grove. Only neighbors share a path. Show the full banner order before dealing. The flag travels Bridge → Watchtower → Grove → Watchtower → Bridge. Stop as soon as the match is decided. Mark the current site with the gold flag and label the next site “Next.” Whenever both players have two banners at the start of a battle, show “Final battle” and hide the Next marker. This can happen as early as battle three. If only one player has two, a next site is still possible. A returning location scores as a fresh battle. Earlier banners belong permanently to the players who earned them. Both players see the same geography regardless of viewing angle.
3. Shuffle each deck independently. Draw four. If an opening hand has no character, set it aside, draw one random character from the remaining deck, return the set-aside cards, shuffle, and draw three more. Begin with an empty board and zero banners each.
4. Choose the opening player randomly. Alternate the opener by battle. In a rematch, the player who opened battle one of the previous match goes second in battle one. Reset all cards, scores, and board positions.

### An open turn

Choose exactly one action. Preview it, then confirm. You can change any selection until confirmation. A rejected or canceled action spends nothing.

| Action | Rule |
|---|---|
| Deploy | Put one character from your hand at any location, including one already scored. Its strength counts immediately. |
| Cast | Play one spell from your hand with all required legal targets. Apply its text once and discard it. |
| Move | Move one of your characters to a neighboring location. Fox Scout may move to either other location. A character may move on successive turns. |
| Swap | Set aside one hand card. Draw one replacement, then discard the set-aside card. The replacement can be another copy of the same card. Swap requires an available replacement. |
| Pass | End this open turn. Passing uses one of your five turns, even if both players pass. |

Each side may have any number of its characters at a location. Totals remain visible even when the artwork must collapse into a stack. Tap a stack to inspect every piece and choose an exact target. Characters never attack each other directly. More strength wins the contest for the flag.

After using a hand card, refill your hand to four if cards are available. Finish resolving the action before refilling. Moving and passing draw nothing. When a draw is required and the draw pile is empty, shuffle its owner's discard pile to make a new one. If both piles are empty, skip the missing draw. Try to refill both hands after battle cleanup. Never shuffle cards out of a hand, the board, or a locked choice. Each player's 18 individual cards must always be accounted for exactly once.

### Starter deck

All numbers below are first-playtest values. Printed strength is the large number on a character. Current strength includes its active bonuses. All “here” effects apply only to the character's current location.

| Card | Printed strength | Open-play ability |
|---|---:|---|
| Stone Guard | 3 | Opposing spells cannot move this character. Its owner may move it normally. |
| Fox Scout | 2 | Its owner's Move action may take it directly to any other location. |
| Twins | 2 | Gains 2 while its owner's other Twins card is here. Both copies get the bonus. |
| Hill Giant | 4 | Once deployed, it cannot move by any means. |
| Trail Captain | 2 | Gains 1 if at least one other friendly character is here. |
| Outrider | 3 | Gains 1 while it is the only friendly character here. |
| Gust | — | Move one movable enemy character to a location adjacent to its current location. Its owner keeps the card on their side. |
| Rally | — | Give one friendly deployed character +2 until this battle is scored. Multiple casts add together. The bonus moves with the character. |
| March | — | Move one movable friendly character directly to either other location. |

Recalculate Twins, Trail Captain, and Outrider whenever the board changes. Each ability checks the presence or identity of pieces, so no strength calculation depends on another strength calculation. Show temporary bonuses on the affected piece. A spell with no legal target stays in hand. You may swap it or pass.

### The last move and how to win

After exactly ten open actions, freeze the public board and show both players' current totals. Each player secretly locks either one character from hand or Hold. Hold adds zero and is always legal. A locked choice is final. Hide both choices until both locks exist. The public hand count, discards, draw count and history stay at their pre-lock values until reveal. Hold and playing a card look identical to the other player during the wait.

Reinforcements contribute only their printed number at the active flag. They never join the board, trigger an ability, change an Outrider's solo bonus, or pair with a deployed Twin. Show the contribution before the player locks it. Both players see the same reveal and score breakdown.

1. Add each player's reinforcement to their current strength at the flag. Higher total earns one banner.
2. On equality, compare each player's combined current board strength at the other two locations, including previously scored locations. Include active bonuses. Higher total earns one banner.
3. If both comparisons tie, award one banner to each. Apply both awards together. If both players reach three, the match is a draw. If only one reaches three, that player wins, including on a shared banner.
4. If play continues, discard all pieces at the scored location and both used reinforcements to their owners' piles. Clear every Rally bonus everywhere. Keep other pieces in place, refill hands, and advance the flag and opener. On a match-ending score, save the frozen board, revealed cards, totals and award as the final view. End before cleanup or further draws.

A banner already awarded can never be taken back. Every battle awards at least one, so the match finishes in three to five completed battles during ordinary play. A battle can end 0–0 at the flag even if both players have forces elsewhere. Apply the same tiebreak. Show the reason on the board before advancing. For example, “Bridge tied 9–9. You won the banner because your other locations led 5–3.” For a complete tie, show “Both players earn a banner” and update both counters together. Preserve each battle’s location, score breakdown, and banner awards in the match history so the two Watchtower results remain distinguishable.

### Phone and multiplayer flow

Use the shared game top bar in all three modes. Keep Pause and the existing controls, and add a consistently placed, accessible question-mark button labeled “How to Play” as a standard for every game. For Banner Clash, it opens the one-minute rules, a scoring example, and the card reference. Closing help returns to the current match. Distinguish flag, player ownership, next location, and legal targets with icons or text as well as color. Show “Your turn 3 of 5” or “Choose your final reinforcement” where both score and hand remain visible.

- **Solo.** Offer a skippable guided opening as a separate practice situation. Teach one choice between contesting the current flag and deploying to the next site. Show which pieces retire and which carry forward. Introduce Hold as an option. A fresh standard deal starts the scored match. The board and Solo opponent use the same upcoming-site rule. At 2–2 there is no upcoming site. The computer evaluates the current flag and the combined strength at the other two sites for the tiebreak. In earlier battles it may also prepare for the next flag. The computer follows the same rules and can see only its own hand and public information. Lock its reinforcement before it receives the human choice. Pause or help suspends computer play.
- **Pass & Play.** Cover the whole private view before every handoff, including the final lock. Name the next player and require a tap to uncover their hand. Return from background to the covered screen. The player who took the tenth open action privately locks first before handing over the device. Give the other player the same frozen board and hide the first choice. Once both choices lock, show a public reveal with both hands concealed. Hand the device to the new opener before showing private cards.
- **Online.** Use the platform's game invitation and resume flow. Both clients must agree on the rules edition before dealing. Decline incompatible editions with an update prompt. Resume existing matches under the edition recorded at setup. Persist the match, private hand, and any pending move before acknowledging it. Bind each action to its match, numbered battle, actor, phase, and turn. Watchtower in battles two and four has distinct action history. Give each copy of a card a stable identity across draws, moves, and reshuffles. Duplicate delivery reuses the recorded outcome. Out-of-order delivery waits for missing events. On reconnect, keep the last acknowledged state and any saved unacknowledged outgoing event. Retry that exact event with its original ID. Save the accepted event, its effects (including private draws), and its acknowledgment in one durable transaction before replying. An identical retry returns the recorded acknowledgment, including after the match ends. Reuse of an event ID with different contents triggers reconciliation. Rebuilding a view uses the saved draw and shuffle outcomes so reconnect cannot redraw a hand. Do not offer a replacement action while the original is pending. Rebuild the current view from accepted events and replay the opponent’s last public move once.
- **Private reveal online.** Exchange a commitment to each choice, including Hold, before either device sends its choice. Bind the commitment to the match and battle and include fresh private randomness so the small set of cards cannot be guessed from the commitment. Persist the choice and randomness before sending. Reveal after both commitments exist. Each device checks its own selection against its frozen hand. The opponent verifies the revealed choice matches the recorded commitment and is a legal reinforcement type or Hold. This casual peer-play design assumes honest dealing on each device. A commitment verifies a locked choice, but cannot independently prove ownership in an unseen hand. Record the result and score exactly once. An invalid reveal or mismatched state suspends scoring and triggers recovery from the agreed history. If recovery cannot restore a valid reveal, show “Match could not be resumed” and let the player close the game as unfinished. Award no banner or win from the recovery error. Never expose the opposing hand or future draw order through the replay.
- **Leaving.** Local pause saves and suspends. Online pause and help affect only the viewer. Online has no turn deadline. Closing the app, a lost connection, or waiting on a reveal preserves the match. Let either player leave the waiting screen and resume later. If one peer never returns, the game stays unfinished. Explicit resignation requires confirmation and gives the opponent the win. For online play, the match inviter orders competing requests to end the match and records the first valid ending once. If both valid final reveals are already recorded there, resolve their score before processing a resignation. A previously accepted resignation ends play. Crossing resignations resolve in the inviter’s recorded acceptance order. Until the decision arrives, show the request as pending. Persist that decision and reuse it on retries. A rematch uses a new match ID so old messages cannot close the new game. Once a result is confirmed it is final. Rematch creates a separate match and requires both online players to accept.

### Pre-build checks and playtest criteria

| Situation checked on paper | Required outcome |
|---|---|
| Flag 7–5, reinforcements 2–4 | Both reach 9. Compare the other two locations before cleanup. |
| A lone Outrider is reinforced by Twins | Outrider keeps its +1. Twins adds only its printed 2. |
| A rallied Twin moves away from its partner | Both lose their pair bonus immediately. Rally stays on the moving Twin through scoring. |
| Gust targets a Guard or Giant | Neither is a legal target. Preview explains why and offers another target or Cancel. |
| A player holds four spells during the reveal | Hold is available. The player keeps all four spells. |
| Both players pass throughout and hold | Award a shared banner. Repeating this gives 2–2 after battle two. Battle three ends in a 3–3 draw. |
| Score was 1–0 before a shared banner | Simultaneous awards produce 2–1. Continue. From 2–0 the same awards produce 3–1 and end the match. |
| A location scored earlier has newly deployed pieces | Their strength counts toward the other-location tiebreak. |
| Connection drops after the first lock or after the score arrives twice | Resume with the same choice. Reveal only after both locks. Award and discard once. |
| Returning to a saved Pass & Play match | A named handoff screen appears before any private hand or locked choice. |
| Fourth battle returns to Watchtower | Score only current pieces and reinforcements. Show the earlier Watchtower banner in the match score. |
| Final battle begins at 2–2 | Hide the next-site marker. The other locations still break a flag tie. One awarded banner ends the match 3–2. A shared banner ends it 3–3. |

### Final rules audit

An independent scoring calculation was checked against the simulator after every open action on a new seed. The audit covered 8,100 matches and 33,714 battles across planning, flag-focused, random-action, and all-pass opponents. All nine cards, movement, swapping, and passing occurred. The audit checked each player’s two copies of every card type and the four-card hand limit after every open action. All 100 mutual pass-and-hold matches drew 3–3 after three battles.

All 63 possible completed sequences of single and shared banner awards terminate in three to five battles. A shared banner can bring a match to 2–2 early. The final-battle display follows the score. A battle beginning at 2–1 can also decide the match, but its next site remains visible because the trailing player can force another battle.

In 2,000 fresh mirror matches, 407 ended in three battles, 885 in four, and 708 in five. The match opener won 987, the other player won 955, and 58 drew. The reveal changed the lead or tie status in 1,793 of 8,301 battles. Neither this result nor the earlier sample warrants a rules adjustment for opening seat.

Keep the frozen public score and both reveal values in the final result even when cleanup would change bonuses. Device acceptance checks must include interruption immediately before and after locking, revealing, scoring and advancing, duplicated and reordered moves at a revisited site, and backgrounding during a private handoff. On a small phone, verify that players can inspect overlapping pieces, choose either Twins copy with a distinct Rally bonus, and read the tiebreak while the full score is visible.

### Follow-up verification

A fresh-seed rerun of the same rule simulator covered 8,100 matches and 33,787 battles. The scoring and card checks passed again. A separate exhaustive check confirmed that the Final battle label is correct in all 14 reachable combinations of banner score and battle number. It appears exactly when every possible award ends the match.

The follow-up review added two online device tests. If the receiver applies a move and its acknowledgment is lost, reconnect must retry the same move and recover the acknowledgment without applying it again. If a committed reinforcement cannot be recovered, the match remains unfinished and the player can leave the error screen. Neither case was exercised on actual devices by the match simulator.

The secret reinforcement can answer a late spell only with additional printed strength. Watch the last open move in human playtests, especially whether late Gust plays feel decisive too often. Any adjustment should preserve the single-step reveal and come after observing actual play.

### Final regression check

Earlier simulations let the planning opponent value a later site during an early 2–2 deciding battle. Its action was legal, but its objective disagreed with the board. The corrected model removes that future-site incentive and retains the other sites’ tiebreak value. Across 8,100 matches, 890 reached a deciding battle before battle five. All 33,711 battles passed the existing scoring and card-accounting checks. Completed matches lasted three to five battles.

For the first online build, interrupt the receiver immediately before and after the durable save of a card play that triggers a shuffle. Retry the original event and compare the hand, deck order, turn, and public board with an uninterrupted control. Repeat for a match-ending score. Repeated delivery must produce the same outcome on both devices.

### Earlier playtest and remaining checks

The revised route and three-banner target were tested in 4,800 seeded heuristic matches. Every match ended within three to five battles and the simulator checked both copies of every card type after each cleanup. Each of three matchups ran 1,600 times with the strategies alternating opening seats.

| Matchup | Three battles | Four | Five | Outcome |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| Identical forward-planning players | 331 | 727 | 542 | Opening player won 761, other player won 788, 51 draws |
| Forward planning with selective Hold against current-flag focus | 306 | 712 | 582 | Forward planner won 1,020, lost 535, drew 45 |
| Both plan ahead, only one holds when the flag result is certain | 299 | 736 | 565 | Selective Hold won 792, lost 756, drew 52 |

In the identical-strategy matches the reveal changed the lead or tie status in 1,435 of 6,611 battles. Flag scores tied 883 times. Keep the on-board explanation and let players inspect the score before cleanup. Treat deployment as the tutorial’s main strategic choice. The selective-Hold comparison gives little reason to teach saving a character as a general rule.

**Pacing.** Target 12–15 minutes of active play, with typical decisive wins lasting at least ten minutes. Three to five battles span 30–50 open actions and three to five reveals. Each human in Solo makes 18–30 decisions. At the simulated mirror-match average of 4.13 battles, a 12-minute Solo match would require roughly 29 seconds per decision including visible resolution time. Record median active time and the share under ten minutes for each mode, separately tracking three-battle finishes. Exclude invitation delays, disconnected waiting and time spent away. Avoid padding the clock with forced waits.

**Human checks before release.** Observe whether new players can explain a win and a tiebreak unaided. On the fourth battle, see if they recognize the returning flag and can identify which pieces survived. Check if repeated Watchtower fights make the opening predictable. Validate privacy at handoff and reconnect on real devices. Simulations measure rule outcomes under the chosen strategies. Human enjoyment, device behavior and elapsed play time remain to be tested.

### Reverse and cross-perspective review

Working backward from every terminal banner score produced exactly the same 63 award histories as forward enumeration. Swapping the players preserved every history. There are 15 possible three-battle award histories, 30 four-battle histories and 18 five-battle histories. These counts describe possible paths, not their likelihood.

The review checked 10,000 combinations of flag margin, other-site margin and printed reinforcement values from both seats, plus all 729 possible friendly character-count combinations at one site with a sample of Rally bonuses. All 3,060 four-card opening selections were classified. Fifteen are all spells, and every one leaves characters available for the guaranteed-character deal.

An abstract model of the reveal gate handled 2,520 orderings of two commitments and two valid reveals delivered twice each. It awarded once and only after all four facts were present. This check assumes valid events and an intact event record. It does not exercise the app’s storage, transport, authentication, or UI.

Earlier match simulations counted cards by type, selected most reinforcements by simple strength heuristics, and ran cleanup even after computing the last award. Their results support the score and match-length bounds. The implementation must separately verify physical card identity, the preserved final view, hand secrecy during lock, retry after restart, and competing end requests. Test those through the normal entry points in all applicable modes. Human timing and enjoyment remain release checks.
